# Understanding Domains and Events

> Domains and events are the foundation of everything in Flowti. This tutorial explains what they are, why they matter, and how they work together to build a living picture of your organization.

---

## What Is a Domain?

A domain is simply a named area of responsibility. It is the part of your world that you know best and want to document. Think of it as a label for a business function, a team, or a process.

Examples of domains:

- **Customer Onboarding** — the steps that bring a new customer from sign-up to first success
- **Order Fulfillment** — receiving orders, checking inventory, and shipping
- **Supplier Management** — qualifying, contracting, and evaluating suppliers
- **IT Service Desk** — handling incidents, changes, and service requests

You do not need to define a domain in technical terms. Just give it the name you use in conversation with your colleagues. The system does not judge your naming — it only needs a starting point.

### Creating a Domain

Open the command palette and search for **Open event catalog**. Navigate to the **Domains** tab and click the **+** button. Give your domain a name and a short description. That is all it takes to plant the first seed.

---

## What Are Events?

Events are things that happen in your domain. They are the verbs — the actions, transitions, and milestones that move work forward. Every domain has them, whether you have written them down or not.

Examples of events in an Order Fulfillment domain:

- Order placed
- Payment received
- Inventory checked
- Shipment dispatched
- Delivery confirmed

In Flowti, events serve two purposes. First, they document what happens in your domain so that anyone reading your vault understands the flow of work. Second, the system uses events internally to keep everything connected and up to date.

### Domain Events vs System Events

There is an important distinction. **Domain events** are the ones you create — they describe your business reality. **System events** are generated by Flowti itself when things happen inside the vault (a file is created, a session starts, settings change). You will mostly work with domain events. System events run quietly in the background.

---

## How Events Flow Between Services

Flowti is built around an internal messaging system called the **EventBus**. When something happens — you create a note, import data, start a session — an event is emitted. Other parts of the system listen for events they care about and react accordingly.

Here is a practical example:

1. You import a CSV of customer orders (see [[Importing CSV Data]])
2. The import creates notes in your vault
3. Each new note triggers a `file.created` event
4. If you have set up an **Event Definition** (see [[Creating Your First Event Definition]]), the system maps that file event to a domain event like `order.received`
5. The Event Catalog updates to show the new event
6. Any dashboard tracking orders refreshes automatically (see [[Building Dashboards]])

You did not wire any of this together manually. You described your domain, set up a definition, and the system handled the rest.

---

## The Event Catalog

The **Event Catalog** is where you browse everything in one place. Open it from the command palette or the sidebar. It has several tabs:

- **Dashboard** — an overview of your documented domains, entities, and events
- **Events** — every event the system knows about, searchable and filterable
- **Domains** — your business domains and their descriptions
- **Health** — a score showing how complete your documentation is

The Health tab is especially useful. It will tell you which domains are well-documented and which still have gaps. Think of it as a compass, not a grade.

---

## Entities — The Nouns of Your Domain

Alongside events, you will also describe **entities** — the things you work with. If events are verbs, entities are nouns. Orders, Customers, Invoices, Products, Suppliers.

Listing your entities helps the system understand the vocabulary of your domain. When you later build queries or dashboards (see [[Creating Analytics Queries]]), the system already knows the shape of your data because you described it here.

---

## Putting It All Together

The power of domains and events reveals itself over time. Each domain you create, each event you name, each entity you list becomes a node in a growing network of meaning. The Event Catalog shows the big picture. The Health score guides your next steps. And the EventBus keeps everything synchronized without you lifting a finger.

Start small. Name one domain. List three events. Describe two entities. Come back tomorrow and add more. The system is patient — and it remembers everything.
