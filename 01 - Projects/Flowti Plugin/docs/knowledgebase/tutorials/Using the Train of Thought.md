# Using the Train of Thought

> A Train of Thought is a rapid-fire way to capture a sequence of ideas, decisions, and observations as linked notes. This tutorial explains how to start a train, add thoughts, and review everything on a visual canvas.

---

## What Is a Train of Thought?

Sometimes your brain is moving faster than a structured documentation session allows. Ideas are flowing, connections are forming, and you need to get them down before they evaporate. That is what the Train of Thought is for.

A train is a **sequence of linked notes** created in rapid succession. Each thought becomes its own note, automatically linked to the one before it. When you are done, the train generates a **visual canvas** showing all your thoughts connected in the order you captured them.

Think of it as a brainstorming session with automatic structure. You provide the ideas — the system handles the linking and visualization.

---

## Starting a Train

There are several ways to begin:

1. Open the command palette and search for **Start train of thoughts**
2. Open the **Train Hub** (search for **Open train hub** in the command palette) and start from there

When you start a train, a simple input appears. Type your first thought — a sentence, a phrase, a question — and press Enter. The first note is created. Now type the next thought. And the next.

Each thought is saved as a separate note in your vault with frontmatter that links it to the train and to the previous thought. You do not need to create links manually. The system chains everything together for you.

---

## During a Train

While a train is running, your workflow is simple: think, type, enter, repeat.

A few things to keep in mind:

- **Keep thoughts short.** Each thought should be a single idea, observation, or question. If you find yourself writing a paragraph, you are probably combining multiple thoughts. Split them up.
- **Do not edit.** This is capture mode, not editing mode. Get the ideas down. You can refine them later.
- **Let the sequence flow naturally.** Thoughts do not need to be perfectly organized. The order they come in is the order they will appear on the canvas. That natural flow often reveals connections you would not have noticed in a rigid outline.

You can also use a Train of Thought within a [[Working with Sessions|session]]. Choose the "Train of Thought" session type, and your train runs inside a timed session with all the session features (timer, goals, activity tracking).

---

## Pausing and Resuming

If you need to step away in the middle of a train, you can pause it. Your progress is saved — all thoughts captured so far are preserved as notes in your vault.

To resume, open the command palette and search for **Resume paused train**. You pick up right where you left off, and new thoughts continue the chain from the last one you captured.

---

## Completing a Train

When the ideas stop flowing, complete the train. Open the command palette and search for **Complete current train**, or use the completion button in the Train Hub.

Completing a train does two things:

1. **Saves the final state** — the train is marked as completed and timestamped
2. **Generates a canvas** — an Obsidian canvas file is created showing all your thoughts as nodes, connected in sequence

The canvas is a visual map of your thinking process. Open it to see the flow of ideas laid out spatially. From here, you can:

- Rearrange nodes to group related thoughts
- Add new nodes for ideas that come up during review
- Draw additional connections between thoughts that relate to each other but were not sequential
- Use the canvas as a starting point for more structured documentation

---

## The Train Hub

The **Train Hub** is your home base for all things train-related. Open it from the command palette with **Open train hub**. Here you will find:

- **Active trains** — any trains currently in progress or paused
- **Completed trains** — your history of finished trains, each linked to its canvas
- **Quick actions** — buttons to start, resume, or complete a train

You can also open the **train timeline** in the sidebar. Search for **Open train timeline sidebar** in the command palette. The timeline shows a chronological view of your thought capture activity — useful for reviewing what you captured and when.

---

## Viewing the Canvas

After completing a train, open the canvas to review your work. Search for **Open train canvas** in the command palette, or navigate to the canvas file from the Train Hub.

The canvas renders each thought as a card, connected to the next in sequence. This visual representation often reveals patterns that are invisible in a linear list:

- Clusters of related thoughts that should become a single document
- Questions that need answers before you can proceed
- Decision points where your thinking branched in multiple directions

Use the canvas as a launchpad. Turn clusters into proper documentation notes. Answer the questions in dedicated notes. Record the decisions with rationale. The raw material from your train becomes the foundation for structured, high-quality documentation.

---

## Tips for Effective Trains

**Lower your quality bar.** A train is for capturing, not perfecting. Typos are fine. Incomplete sentences are fine. Get the thought down and move on.

**Set a time limit.** Even without a formal session, give yourself a boundary. Ten minutes of focused thought capture is usually enough to fill a meaningful canvas.

**Review the same day.** The canvas is most valuable while the context is fresh. Spend a few minutes after completing a train to organize and annotate the thoughts.

**Use trains for meetings.** When you are in a meeting and ideas are flying, start a train. Each key point becomes a note. After the meeting, the canvas is your structured summary.
