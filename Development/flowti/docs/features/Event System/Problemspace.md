# Problem Space — Reacting to Incoming Information

## One-Sentence Problem Statement

> **I need my system to recognize and react to meaningful events in my information flow, so incoming data does not become noise, risk, or manual work.**

## Context

I use Obsidian as my central place to think, plan, and work.

Information reaches me every day from many directions:

- reports from other systems
    
- files synced from shared folders
    
- exports from tools I don’t control
    
- notes and documents created by others
    
- automated drops that happen whether I am ready or not
    

All of this ends up in my vault.

What I actually care about, however, is **not the files themselves**, but **what they mean**.

---

## The Core Problem

### Files arrive, but meaning does not

When new files appear in my vault:

- I have to notice them
    
- I have to open them
    
- I have to decide what they represent
    
- I have to remember what to do next
    

The system does not help me with this.

A file being created does not tell me:

- _why_ it exists
    
- _what kind_ of thing it represents
    
- _what should happen_ because of it
    

So I end up doing the same mental work over and over again.

---

## The Ongoing Pain

### I repeatedly ask myself:

- “Is this new or have I already processed it?”
    
- “Is this important or just noise?”
    
- “Do I need to react to this now?”
    
- “What did I do last time a file like this arrived?”
    

The more external systems I connect, the worse this gets.

What starts as “convenient syncing” slowly turns into:

- clutter
    
- uncertainty
    
- missed actions
    
- duplicated work
    

---

## High-Volume Makes It Worse

Sometimes many files arrive at once:

- daily or weekly reports
    
- batch exports
    
- historical data
    
- delayed syncs
    

In these moments:

- I cannot tell what has already been handled
    
- I cannot safely process everything manually
    
- I worry about missing something important
    

If files arrive while I am not even using Obsidian, the problem compounds:

- I don’t know what changed
    
- I don’t know what needs attention
    
- I don’t know where to start
    

---

## Why Current Approaches Fall Short

### Manual workflows don’t scale

I can manually move, tag, or link files—but only when volume is low.

### Folder structures are not enough

Folders tell me _where_ something is, not _what happened_.

### File names are fragile signals

Naming conventions help, but they are:

- easy to break
    
- hard to enforce
    
- difficult to evolve
    

### Automation without clarity feels dangerous

If things happen automatically without me understanding why, I stop trusting the system.

---

## What I Actually Want

I want the system to help me **recognize and react to what happened**, not just show me new files.

I want to think in terms of:

- “A daily report arrived”
    
- “New data was delivered”
    
- “Something relevant just happened”
    

Not:

- “A file was created in a folder”
    

---

## Jobs to Be Done (User Perspective)

### JTBD 1 — Recognize Meaningful Arrivals

**When** new information enters my vault  
**I want** the system to recognize when it represents something meaningful  
**So that** I don’t have to interpret every file manually

---

### JTBD 2 — Decide What I Care About

**When** different kinds of things happen  
**I want** to decide explicitly which ones matter to me  
**So that** I am not overwhelmed by noise

---

### JTBD 3 — React Consistently

**When** something I care about happens again  
**I want** the same reaction to happen every time  
**So that** I don’t rely on memory or discipline

---

### JTBD 4 — Trust That Nothing Is Missed

**When** many things arrive at once  
**Or** when I was not present  
**I want** the system to catch up reliably  
**So that** I know nothing important slipped through unnoticed

---

### JTBD 5 — Avoid Doing the Same Work Twice

**When** files are re-synced, renamed, or reintroduced  
**I want** the system to recognize what I already handled  
**So that** I don’t repeat work or create duplicates

---

### JTBD 6 — Understand What Happened and Why

**When** the system reacts automatically  
**I want** to understand what triggered that reaction  
**So that** I can trust and adjust the system over time

---

### JTBD 7 — Grow My System Gradually

**When** my needs evolve  
**I want** to add more reactions over time  
**So that** my system grows with me instead of collapsing under complexity

---

## Desired Outcome

When information flows into my vault:

- I feel **in control**
    
- I understand **what happened**
    
- I trust that the right things were handled
    
- I can focus on decisions instead of triage
    

The system should help me move from:

> “Something changed… I need to check.”

to:

> “This happened, and it was handled as expected.”
